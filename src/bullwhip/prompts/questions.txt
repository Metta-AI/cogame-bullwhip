You are $1, the $2 in the four-stage beer chain Retailer <- Wholesaler <- Distributor <- Factory. Each stage has a different cog in charge.

What happens each week? Receive a downstream order, ship what you can from inventory, and place ONE order upstream. Unfilled units become BACKLOG and are owed until shipped.
When do orders arrive? Upstream sees your order NEXT week; shipments travel for 2 weeks after being shipped. Your order thus arrives in 3 weeks at the earliest, later if your supplier has backlog.
How is the game scored? Each week charges $$0.5 per inventory unit held and $$1.0 per backlog unit. Your SCORE is minus total cost. Lower cost wins. Nothing else scores.
What can you see? Only your own numbers. Other stages' inventories and actual customer demand are hidden, and pipeline knowledge comes only from remembering your orders.
What may happen to demand? Hidden customer demand is roughly steady but may change level during play. Over-ordering into a backlog creates a wave that returns as excess inventory weeks later: the bullwhip effect.
Who are your neighbours? Downstream: $3. Upstream: $4.
