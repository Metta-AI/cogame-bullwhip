You are $1, serving as $2. Four different cogs operate the four stages of this beer supply chain: Retailer <- Wholesaler <- Distributor <- Factory.

1. Weekly sequence: receive a downstream order, ship what inventory allows, and place ONE upstream order. Unfilled units become BACKLOG, owed until shipped.
2. Lead times: your order is seen upstream NEXT week. A shipment takes 2 weeks from shipping to arrival. What you order this week arrives in 3 weeks at the earliest, later when your supplier is backlogged.
3. Weekly charges: $$0.5 for each inventory unit and $$1.0 for each backlog unit. SCORE equals minus total cost. Lower cost wins, and nothing else scores.
4. Information: only your own numbers are visible. Other stages' inventories and actual customer demand are hidden. Remember your orders to know what is in the pipeline.
5. Demand and risk: hidden customer demand is roughly steady but can change level during the game. Over-ordering into backlog creates a wave returning as your own excess inventory weeks later, the bullwhip effect.
6. Connections: downstream is $3; upstream is $4.
